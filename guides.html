1. How to Calculate the Price of Gold Jewellery in India

Many first-time buyers only multiply weight by the daily 22K rate, then feel surprised by the final showroom invoice. In India, a standard jewellery bill combines three components: net gold value, making charges, and 3% GST.

Final Bill = (Weight × 22K Rate + Making Charges) × 1.03

For example, on an 8g (1 Pavan) chain at ₹12,000/g with 10% making charges: Net Gold is ₹96,000, Making is ₹9,600 (subtotal ₹1,05,600), and 3% GST adds ₹3,168—bringing the total bill to ₹1,08,768. You can run any custom budget or weight instantly on the GoldBuddy Calculator.

2. 22K vs 24K Gold: Which Should You Buy?

24K gold (99.9% purity) is soft and malleable, making it ideal for coins, bullion bars, and digital gold savings—but too soft for durable everyday ornaments. 22K gold (91.6% gold mixed with 8.4% strengthening alloys like copper or silver) is the Indian standard for hallmarked jewellery.

Choose 24K if your goal is pure value storage or gifting coins without high making charges. Choose 22K (916 HUID) when buying rings, bangles, necklaces, or wedding sets meant to be worn.

3. What Is 1 Pavan of Gold in Grams?

Across Tamil Nadu, Kerala, and much of South India, jewellery weights are commonly quoted in Pavan (also called Sovereign or Savaran). One Pavan equals exactly 8 grams of gold.

For quick mental math: Half Pavan = 4g, 1 Pavan = 8g, 5 Pavan = 40g, and 10 Pavan = 80g. GoldBuddy automatically shows the 8g (1 Pavan) 22K price right under the 22K card.

4. How GST Is Calculated on Gold Jewellery

Under India's GST framework, physical gold and gold jewellery attract a uniform 3% Goods and Services Tax. Importantly, 3% GST applies to the combined total of gold value plus making charges—not just the gold weight alone.

If your gold value is ₹50,000 and making charges are ₹5,000 (₹55,000 subtotal), 3% GST equals ₹1,650, making the final invoice ₹56,650. Always check whether a quoted rate is "with GST" or "without GST" using GoldBuddy's header toggle.

5. How Jewellery Making Charges & Wastage Work

Making charges cover craftsmanship, design complexity, and polishing. Showrooms quote them either as a percentage of gold value (typically 6%–18% for plain gold, 18%–28% for intricate antique or temple designs) or a flat ₹ per gram fee.

Note that when you exchange or sell old jewellery later, you recover the net gold weight value—making charges and GST are not refunded. Comparing making charges across two jewellers can save ₹400–₹1,200 per gram on heavy wedding sets.

6. Gold Coin vs. Gold Jewellery as an Investment

If you are accumulating gold primarily for future resale or conversion rather than immediate wear, 24K 999.9 gold coins or bars carry much lower making charges (often 2%–5%) compared to jewellery (10%–25%).

Jewellery makes sense when you want utility and cultural wearability, whereas coins or staggered digital gold allow you to lock in pure metal weight with minimal upfront overhead.

7. How to Check BIS Hallmark (HUID) on Gold

In India, genuine hallmarked gold jewellery carries three laser-etched marks under the mandatory BIS HUID standard:

  • BIS Logo (triangle mark)
  • Purity Grade (e.g., 22K916 for 91.6% gold or 18K750 for 75%)
  • 6-Character Alphanumeric HUID Code (unique to that specific piece)

Before paying, enter that 6-character HUID code in the official BIS Care App ("Verify HUID") to confirm the jeweller's registration, article type, and certified purity.

8. How to Calculate Gold Price Per Gram

Global gold trades in US Dollars per troy ounce (1 troy ounce = 31.1035 grams). To convert global spot prices into an Indian landed 24K per-gram baseline, multiply the global XAU/USD rate by USD/INR, divide by 31.1035, and factor in Indian import duty, local bullion premiums, and 3% GST.

For 22K jewellery, multiply the 24K base rate by 22 / 24 (0.9167). GoldBuddy handles this conversion automatically on every refresh.

9. When Is the Right Time to Buy Gold?

No tool can reliably predict exact short-term tops or bottoms in global bullion markets. Rather than waiting indefinitely for a "perfect" bottom, experienced buyers focus on two practical habits: comparing today's rate against its recent 5-day or 30-day average, and avoiding panic-buying 100% of a budget during sudden single-day spikes.

10. Gold Price in India: What Affects Daily Prices?

Indian retail gold rates move daily based on four main forces: international spot gold (XAU/USD), the USD/INR exchange rate (a weaker Rupee makes imported gold costlier even if global gold is flat), central bank & ETF demand, and seasonal Indian festival/wedding demand.

11. How Much Gold Can ₹50,000 Buy?

Because showroom bills include making charges and 3% GST, ₹50,000 buys slightly less net gold weight than 50,000 ÷ 22K rate. At ₹12,000/g with 10% making charges and 3% GST, effective cost is ₹13,596/g—meaning ₹50,000 buys roughly 3.677 grams of 22K jewellery. Test your exact budget in the GoldBuddy Budget Calculator.

12. How to Plan Gold Purchases Using Staggered Buying

Staggered buying (similar to a Systematic Investment Plan) splits a planned purchase—such as ₹1,50,000 for a family wedding—into 3 parts (30% / 40% / 30%) across slightly lower target price levels. If prices dip further after your first purchase, your remaining budget buys more grams at a lower average cost.
